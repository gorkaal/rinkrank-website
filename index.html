How PuckLadder power rankings are calculated

Independent ratings based on completed GTHL league games

Power Rank orders teams by their Power Rating, highest first. League Rank comes from GTHL standings and reflects league points. Power Rating measures something different: game score margins adjusted for the strength of opponents.

  1. Start with completed league games. We use every completed game in the division and reconcile each team's games played, wins, losses, ties, goals for and goals against with GTHL-published standings. If they do not match, no new rankings are published.
  2. Limit blowouts. Goal difference is capped at +6 or −6 per game. A 9–1 win and a 6–0 win each contribute +6; a 4–2 win contributes +2. Losses contribute negative margins, and ties contribute zero.
  3. Adjust for opponent quality. Each team's starting value is its average capped goal margin. An iterative calculation adds the average ratings of its opponents. This gives more credit for a comparable result against a strong opponent than against a weaker one. Each of a team's games receives equal weight.
  4. Center the ratings and rank teams. The process repeats until the ratings stabilize, centering them so the average team in the division has a rating of zero. Positive ratings are above the division average and negative ratings below. The highest-rated team receives Power Rank 1.
  5. Measure Momentum separately. The same opponent-adjusted calculation is run using each team's four most recent games (or fewer if not yet played). Momentum measures recent form; it does not change the full-season Power Rank.

Power Ratings are PuckLadder's independent estimates, not league rankings or guarantees of future results. League points and win–loss records are displayed for comparison but are not added directly to the Power Rating calculation.

PuckLadder is independent of the GTHL. GTHL schedule · GTHL standings. Rankings update only when collected game scores reconcile with published league standings.
